# Astronomical engine

Code: `src/lib/eclipse/` — `elements.ts` (data), `circumstances.ts` (local circumstances), `path.ts` (path geometry), `index.ts` (façade bound to the 2027 eclipse and the adopted ΔT).

## Input data

Polynomial Besselian elements of the total solar eclipse of 2027 August 2, published by F. Espenak (NASA GSFC), t0 = 10:00 TDT, ephemerides VSOP87/ELP2000-82, lunar radius k1 = 0.272488 (penumbra), k2 = 0.272281 (umbra). Source: <https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEsearch/SEdata.php?Ecl=+20270802> (consulted 2026-10-25). Greatest eclipse: 10:06:34 UT (ΔT 76 s), 25.5° N 33.2° E, duration 6 min 23 s, gamma 0.1421, magnitude 1.0790, Saros 136.

## Method

Explanatory Supplement to the Astronomical Almanac (1992), chapter 8; J. Meeus, *Elements of Solar Eclipses 1951–2200* (1989).

For an observer at geodetic latitude φ, east longitude λ and height h:

```
U = atan(0.99664719 · tan φ)
ρ sin φ' = 0.99664719 sin U + (h / 6378137) sin φ
ρ cos φ' = cos U + (h / 6378137) cos φ
H = μ(t) + λ − 0.00417807 · ΔT                  (hour angle of the shadow axis, degrees)
ξ = ρ cos φ' sin H
η = ρ sin φ' cos d − ρ cos φ' cos H sin d
ζ = ρ sin φ' sin d + ρ cos φ' cos H cos d
u = x − ξ,  v = y − η,  m = √(u² + v²)
L1 = l1 − ζ tan f1,  L2 = l2 − ζ tan f2
```

- **Maximum**: minimum of m — coarse scan (1 min) then Newton iterations τ = −(u·a + v·b)/(a² + b²), where a, b are the time derivatives of u, v.
- **Contacts**: C1/C4 where m = L1, C2/C3 where m = |L2| (L2 < 0 for a total eclipse), found by bisection (40 iterations → < 0.01 s).
- **Magnitude** = (L1 − m)/(L1 + L2); **Moon/Sun diameter ratio** = (L1 − L2)/(L1 + L2); **obscuration** = area of the intersection of the two disks (radii (L1 + L2)/2 and (L1 − L2)/2, distance m) divided by the area of the solar disk.
- **Sun position**: altitude and azimuth from H and d (direction of the shadow axis ≈ direction of the Sun); geometric altitude, no refraction.
- **Simulation**: position of the Moon relative to the Sun = (u, v) in units of the solar radius, x towards celestial east, y towards celestial north; rotated by the parallactic angle q = atan2(sin H, tan φ cos d − sin d cos H) for a zenith-up view.
- **Path** (`path.ts`): for each meridian, the central line is the latitude minimising m at maximum (golden-section search); limits and isolines (duration, magnitude) are found by bisection on latitude. Generated by `npm run data:path` into `public/data/eclipse-2027-path.geojson` (sampling: every 0.02° of longitude from 6° to 13° E, 0.1° elsewhere).

## Time scales

- Elements are functions of TDT (TT). UT = TT − ΔT. UT1 ≈ UTC (|UT1 − UTC| < 0.9 s).
- Displayed times: UTC and Tunisian time (`Africa/Tunis`, UTC+1 all year; no daylight saving time since 2009).

## ΔT

NASA computed the elements with an extrapolated ΔT = 76.0 s. Measured values have since levelled off (ΔT ≈ 69.1 s in 2025 from IERS Bulletin A: 32.184 s + 37 s − (UT1 − UTC)). The site adopts **ΔT = 69.2 s** (`ADOPTED_DELTA_T`), estimated uncertainty ±1 s.

Effect of ΔT: the path shifts in longitude by 0.00417807° per second of ΔT (≈ 0.38 km per second at 35° N). Between 76.0 s and 69.2 s: ≈ 0.028°, i.e. ≈ 2.6 km **westwards** (for a fixed hour angle H = μ + λ − 0.00417807·ΔT, a smaller ΔT means a smaller λ). The adopted value is applied consistently to the map, the calculator and all tables. Local UT times change by up to a few seconds; the validation against NASA uses NASA's 76.0 s.

## Accuracy and limits

| Source of error | Effect | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Lunar limb profile (mountains/valleys) | Limits shifted by up to 1–2 km; durations near limits change by several seconds; C2/C3 by ~1–2 s elsewhere | Results flagged within 5 km of a limit (`NEAR_LIMIT_KM`) |
| Lunar radius convention (k) | Path width and duration depend on k (NASA k2 = 0.272281) | Documented; comparison with Astronomy Engine published |
| ΔT 2027 (±1 s) | ±0.4 km in longitude, < 1 s on local times | Documented |
| Elevation (sea level assumed) | A few hundred metres on the limits at 500 m altitude, < 1 s on times | Documented; the engine accepts a height |
| Town = single point | A town extends over several km | Calculator for exact points |
| Refraction ignored | < 1′ at altitudes > 50° | Negligible |

See [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) (generated) and `tests/unit/`.

- Against NASA's path table (ΔT 76 s), 08:40–09:40 UT: central durations within 0.2 s, limits within 0.6 km, Sun altitude/azimuth within 1°, Moon/Sun ratio within 0.001, time of maximum within 1 min (table resolution).
- Against Astronomy Engine (same ΔT): C1, C4 and maximum within ~2–3 s for 16 reference towns; totality agrees on the northern side; Astronomy Engine's southern limit lies ~2–4 km further south.
